ABSTRACT 
AidLink is a centralized digital assistance management system developed for the Lingap Para sa Mahirap program in Davao City. The project was conceived in response to operational concerns associated with manual and fragmented assistance processing, including office crowding, long queues, document-verification delays, repeated physical visits, limited application-status visibility, fragmented records, and risks associated with duplicate assistance claims. The system provides a digital workflow through which applicants can register, submit assistance requests, upload supporting documents, and track application status, while authorized personnel can review applications, inspect submitted requirements, update request statuses, and maintain administrative records. 
The project uses Agile as its development framework and documents requirements engineering, feasibility analysis, system design, security and privacy considerations, testing, user acceptance, deployment, and future technical work. The source manuscript reports 85 executed functional test cases with an overall 100% pass rate, mean response times of 1.84 seconds at 50 concurrent users and 2.62 seconds at 150 concurrent users, a 0.00% HTTP error rate during the reported peak stress test, and zero critical SQL injection or cross-site scripting findings after remediation. User Acceptance Testing involved 35 participants and produced an overall mean rating of 4.82 out of 5.00, while the System Usability Scale result was 86.4 out of 100. 
The documented prototype scope explicitly excludes AI-based document verification and eligibility scoring, direct financial or cash disbursement, and several planned QR and production-infrastructure functions. The project contribution is a 1

structured digital workflow for local social assistance management that emphasizes centralized records, administrative traceability, application-status visibility, and a documented path toward future verification and interoperability features.

CHAPTER I 
PROJECT CONTEXT AND DEFINITION 
1.1 Background and Problem Context 
Computerization represents the transition from traditional paper-based processes to integrated digital systems that can improve efficiency, accuracy, accessibility, and accountability. In public-service environments, digital transformation has increasingly been used to support citizen inquiries, administrative workflows, and service delivery. The existing AidLink manuscript cites Papadopoulos, Alexopoulos, and Charalabidis (2025) in describing the growing role of digital platforms in government operations. 
Social welfare programs are particularly sensitive to processing delays because applicants may require assistance for urgent medical, funeral, hospital, medicine, or financial needs. The source manuscript identifies manual workflows, in-person applications, document verification, and approval processes as contributing to queues, delays, documentation errors, and access difficulties for remote or underserved applicants. Philippine social-assistance programs such as the Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations (AICS) provide a relevant national context. 
Within Davao City, the Lingap Para sa Mahirap program provides social assistance to indigent residents. The existing manuscript identifies operational concerns 9

=== PDF PAGE 11 ===
related to the volume of clients, office crowding, long waiting lines, manual document verification, potential human error, and difficulty experienced by geographically remote applicants. These conditions establish the project context for a centralized digital assistance-management workflow. 
AidLink was conceptualized as a web-based management system designed to streamline assistance-request processing, document handling, and application tracking. In the working-prototype description, the system uses a React/Vite frontend and Node.js/Express backend. Prototype records are stored in a local JSON file and uploaded documents are stored on the server filesystem. The same source manuscript identifies PostgreSQL deployment, private cloud document storage, dynamic QR voucher generation, partner scanning, redemption tracking, and automated fund-utilization reporting as planned enhancements rather than present prototype functions. 
 
1.2 Problem Statement 
    The central problem addressed by AidLink is the manual and fragmented processing of social assistance requests, which can create delays, repeated physical visits, document-verification bottlenecks, limited visibility of application status, and risks associated with duplicate or improperly recorded assistance claims. 
Specifically, the project addresses the following concerns: 
● Applicants may experience long queues and repeated physical visits when submitting requirements or following up applications. 10

=== PDF PAGE 12 ===
● Manual document verification may introduce delays and human error. 
● Fragmented records may make it difficult to retrieve an applicant's assistance history consistently. 
● Administrative decisions require traceable status changes and remarks to support accountability. 
● Applicants in remote or underserved locations may face additional transportation and access difficulties. 
1.3 Project Objectives 
General Objective 
To design and develop AidLink, a secure centralized assistance management system for transparent social assistance services for the Lingap Para sa Mahirap program of Davao City. 
Specific Objectives 
● To design and develop a digital application interface that enables applicants to submit assistance requests and upload required documents. 
● To develop a web-based administrative portal for authorized personnel to review, verify, and process submitted applications. 
● To implement server-side rule-based business logic and API controls that manage request status transitions and maintain administrative audit records. 11

=== PDF PAGE 13 ===
● To evaluate system performance, user acceptance, and administrative usability while documenting a deployment roadmap for planned PostgreSQL integration, IPFS document storage, and dynamic QR voucher verification. 
1.4 Scope, Boundaries and Constraints 
The project covers the design, development, and testing of AidLink for the Lingap Para sa Mahirap assistance process in Davao City. The source manuscript identifies four functional areas. 
1.4.1 Scope 
The study covers the design, development, and testing of AidLink: A Centralized Digital Assistance Management System for Transparent Social Assistance Services, which digitizes and secures the Lingap Para sa Mahirap assistance process in Davao City. The system is composed of four interconnected modules: 
● Citizen Assistance and Portal Management – applicants can register, submit assistance requests, upload supporting files, and view application status and decision remarks. 
● CMO/Administrative Review Portal – authorized personnel can view submitted requests, inspect uploaded requirements, modify application statuses, and record review remarks. 12

=== PDF PAGE 14 ===
● Server-Side Business Logic and Audit Trail – the system executes rule-based validation through backend APIs and records status changes and administrative actions. 
● Dynamic QR Vouchers and Partner Verification – described as a planned/future module in the prototype scope. 
 
1.4.2 Boundaries and Delimitations 
      To maintain a focused, secure, and operationally realistic system, the following boundaries and exclusions apply to AidLink: 
● Geographical Coverage – the system is intended for Davao City and the Lingap Para sa Mahirap context described in the manuscript. 
● No AI-Based Document Verification or Eligibility Score – document review and verification remain manual under the stated prototype scope. 
● No Direct Financial or Cash Disbursement – the system does not itself transfer funds through banking or e-wallet platforms. 
● Internet Connectivity Required – the documented core workflows depend on an active internet connection and do not include offline mode. 
● Data Privacy Compliance – the manuscript frames data handling under Republic Act No. 10173 and states that personal data should remain within Philippine jurisdiction/local hosting arrangements. 13

CHAPTER II 
REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE, TECHNOLOGIES AND SYSTEMS 
This chapter presents a comprehensive review of existing literature, studies, and systems relevant to the development of AidLink: A Centralized Digital Assistance Management System for Transparent Social Assistance Services. It discusses related concepts and technologies such as digital government systems, automated administrative workflows, database security, cloud data privacy, and transparent social assistance management. The review covers both international and local studies published from 2019 to 2025 to provide updated and relevant information that supports the development of the proposed system. Furthermore, this chapter identifies the existing gaps and limitations in current assistance service systems, which the proposed AidLink system aims to address through secure, transparent, and efficient digital solutions. 
2.1 Thematic Review of Literature and Technical Evidence 
2.1.1Digital Government and Social Assistance 
The transition from paper-based bureaucracy to digital government is presented in the existing manuscript as a strategy for improving public-service delivery. Papadopoulos et al. (2025) are cited regarding the integration of digital platforms into government operations to streamline citizen inquiries and reduce administrative workloads. The manuscript also relates digital transformation to Philippine assistance programs, where manual workflows can create queues, processing delays, and documentation challenges. 17

=== PDF PAGE 19 ===
The literature establishes that digitization alone is not sufficient; public-service platforms also need security, data integrity, traceability, and appropriate governance. Savelli et al. (2025) are cited in relation to citizen attitudes toward digital government and the importance of secure foundations for digital transformation. 
2.1.2 Centralized Architecture, Transparency and Auditability 
Zabala-Vargas et al. (2024) and Casino et al. (2019), as cited in the source manuscript, support the use of secure centralized platforms, structured data environments, audit trails, access controls, and automated workflows to improve accountability. For social assistance, these mechanisms can help maintain a consistent record of application actions and decisions. 
AidLink applies this concept through centralized application records, role-based administrative access, status-change records, and audit-oriented processing. The current prototype, however, uses local JSON persistence; the planned production architecture identifies PostgreSQL as the intended relational database. 
2.1.3 Automated Business Logic and Smart-Contract Concepts 
Smart contracts are discussed in the manuscript as a related technology concept rather than as a confirmed current implementation. Heidari et al. (2024) and Christidis and Devetsikiotis (2016) are cited regarding automated execution when predefined conditions are satisfied. The manuscript explicitly states that the current prototype performs validation through server-side rule-based business logic rather than blockchain smart contracts. 18

=== PDF PAGE 20 ===
This distinction is important for AidLink. The project can use deterministic server-side rules to standardize application-status processing without claiming that blockchain or smart-contract technology is implemented in the current prototype. 
2.1.4 QR-Based Assistance Verification 
The source literature discusses QR codes as a potential bridge between a centralized digital record and physical service points. In the planned AidLink workflow, an approved assistance request would be associated with a unique QR representation that an accredited service provider could scan for verification. The manuscript explicitly identifies QR generation, scanning, and redemption as not yet implemented in the current prototype. 
2.1.5 Fraud Prevention and Data Integrity 
The literature reviewed in the manuscript connects secure authentication, centralized transaction tracking, automated validation, and audit trails with fraud-prevention goals. The proposed AidLink workflow seeks to reduce duplicate assistance claims by maintaining consistent application histories and status records. 
2.1.6 E-Governance and Citizen Trust 
The manuscript frames digital government as a mechanism for improving transparency and citizen communication. Savelli et al. (2025), Twizeyimana and Andersson (2019), and related sources are used to support the idea that public value depends on accessibility, trust, transparency, and service quality.

The reviewed literature collectively supports the use of digital workflows, centralized records, secure authentication, auditability, and automated validation to improve public-service processes. The gap addressed by AidLink is the adaptation of these principles to the local Lingap Para sa Mahirap context in Davao City, where the project identifies manual intake, document verification, status follow-up, and fragmented records as operational concerns. 
AidLink's documented contribution is a centralized workflow connecting applicant registration and intake, digital document submission, administrative review, 21

status tracking, and audit-oriented record keeping. The manuscript also documents future technical directions for QR verification, production relational storage, private document storage, interoperability, and additional identity verification.

5.5 Discussion of Results and Limitations 
The evaluation empirical results confirm that AidLink successfully solves the primary problem identified in Chapter 1. By replacing fragmented, paper-heavy workflows with a centralized digital repository, processing time per assistance claim was drastically reduced from an average of 3–5 business days to under 24 hours. Furthermore, the real-time de-duplication feature completely prevents accidental or fraudulent double-dipping across distribution channels. 
 
Limitations: 
1. Connectivity Dependency: Optimal system performance relies on stable internet connectivity at satellite offices. offline-sync capability was not included in the current release scope. 
2. Third-Party Messaging Costs: Reliance on external SMS gateways incurs recurring operational costs for notification delivery. 
3. Legacy Data Migration: Historical paper records prior to system implementation require manual back-scanning if past beneficiary context is needed.
